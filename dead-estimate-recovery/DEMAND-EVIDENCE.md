# Demand evidence: are owners actually asking for this?

Every claim on the page traces to a published source below. Gathered 2026-07-12 via web research. Use these when a prospect asks "who says this works."

Honesty note: most of these are reported by third-party marketing and industry blogs, not primary academic research. The PHCC follow-up-cost figure is cited by US Tech Automations, not linked directly to a PHCC page. Present them as "reported by industry sources," which is exactly how the page frames them. If you want a primary source for a stat before leaning on it in a contract or ad, verify it first.

## The money sitting in dead quotes

- Home service owners are sitting on $50,000 to $500,000 in unsold estimates. A 5-tech shop sends 80 to 120 estimates a month, converts only 31% to 38%, and leaves $14,000 to $28,000 unbooked every month. [The AI Trades](https://theaitrades.ai/blog/unsold-estimate-reactivation-campaign-contractors), [US Tech Automations](https://ustechautomations.com/resources/blog/home-service-estimate-follow-up-automation-roi-analysis-2026)
- Home service companies pull 18% to 32% of annual revenue from their existing database without buying a single new lead. [Prestyj](https://prestyj.com/blog/home-services-off-season-revenue-customer-database-2026), [exploreHVAC](https://explorehvac.com/blog/database-reactivation-hvac-home-service-marketing/)

## The follow-up gap (why the money is stuck)

- 43% of estimates never get a single follow-up. Each missed follow-up costs about $147 in lost revenue attribution (PHCC data). [US Tech Automations](https://ustechautomations.com/resources/blog/home-service-estimate-follow-up-automation-roi-analysis-2026)
- 90% of contractors quit after the first or second touch, while 80% of sales require five or more follow-ups. The top quartile of home service companies converts 52% of estimates versus 31% for the median, and the difference is almost entirely follow-up consistency. [ServiceTitan](https://www.servicetitan.com/blog/webinar-recap-follow-ups-101-back-2-basics), [US Tech Automations](https://ustechautomations.com/resources/blog/home-service-estimate-follow-up-automation-roi-analysis-2026)

## It is already a paid market (proof of demand)

- Done-for-you database and estimate reactivation is an established service category with many agencies selling it. An $800 campaign has returned $31,000+ even at a pessimistic 2% reactivation rate. ROI runs 20X or more. [Suparev](https://suparev.com/blog/database-reactivation-hvac-guide), [Relentless Digital](https://www.relentless-digital.com/faq/database-reactivation-faqs)
- Cost per recovered job runs $8 to $40, versus $80 to $240 to acquire the same job from cold lead sources. Full ROI is typically hit within 30 days, with booked revenue often inside 48 hours. [Suparev ROI](https://suparev.com/blog/database-reactivation-roi), [exploreHVAC](https://explorehvac.com/blog/database-reactivation-hvac-home-service-marketing/)
- HVAC contractors are generating $300,000+ from dormant leads using database reactivation. [Prestyj](https://prestyj.com/blog/home-services-off-season-revenue-customer-database-2026)

## The recovery rate we quote (why the calculator uses 10%)

- Reported conversion on dead estimates runs 5% to 12% (Relentless Digital reactivation analysis). About 1 in 5 ghosted quotes comes back within 90 days with polite, predictable follow-up. The page and the audit both use a conservative 10%, the middle of the published range. [Relentless Digital](https://www.relentless-digital.com/faq/database-reactivation-faqs), [Conversion Surgery](https://conversionsurgery.io/blog/sent-the-quote-renovation-sales-die)

## How to use these

- On the landing page: the "numbers behind dead quotes" band cites five of these inline.
- On a call: lead with the owner's own leaked money (the free audit), then cite the $8 to $40 versus $80 to $240 line to frame the fee as cheap money.
- Never quote a made-up number. If a stat is not on this list, do not put it on the page.
